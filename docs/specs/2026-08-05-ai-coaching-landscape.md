# The AI coaching landscape, and where recover stands

**Date:** 2026-08-05 · **Trigger:** the intervals.icu Annual Training Plan
Builder shipped, and the question "what can we use?" turned into a survey of
the whole `forum.intervals.icu/c/ai-tools` category — fifty products in nine
months, several with 20–30 000 views.

**Goal this document serves:** recover is to be the app that does what all of
them do, 100 % free and self-hosted, where every one of them paywalls
something.

## Method

Fifty topics enumerated from the category index. Sixteen read in depth,
including the tails of the largest threads (where churn shows up rather than
launch enthusiasm): IntervalCoach, Montis.icu, LeCoach, Coach Watts, Intervals
Pro, MyTrainPal, PlanWatts, athletedata, Domestique, Section 11, RestOrTrain,
RaceMind, Trace, Kipp, MacroBurn, Plan My Peak. Plus the 166-post Annual
Training Plan Builder thread and the primary sports-science sources behind its
cited defaults.

| Domestique    | free, MIT, localhost         | no LLM in the loop at all                                                |

Two of the "free" products are explicitly funded by a solo developer's card.
That is not a stable state, and the users can see it: **bring-your-own-API-key
is a top unmet request on both Intervals Pro and MyTrainPal**, and Intervals
Pro is already modelling a "~£3/month platform fee + your own keys" tier.

recover shipped that in v0.1 — "AI coach — bring your own key (Anthropic or any
OpenAI-compatible endpoint, Ollama included)". With Ollama the marginal cost is
genuinely zero. **recover does not have to choose to be free; its architecture
makes the cost problem someone else's.**

**Competitive warning.** Coach Watts states "I'll be open-sourcing the code
soon, so for those who want full control over your data, you'll be able to
self-host your own instance." As of the latest posts in that thread it has not
happened. The positioning is contested in words, not yet in shipped code.

**The real competitor is Domestique** — free, MIT, localhost-only, science-based
with inline citations. What it lacks: a conversational coach, proactive push,
and a phone. It is a macOS/Windows desktop app; recover is a PWA that messages
you in the morning.

## 2. The market converged on recover's architecture — independently, four times

> **RaceMind** — "the actual training plan generation is **deliberately not
> generated by an LLM**." The developer tested LLM planning and found it
> unreliable for decisions involving fatigue, progression, injuries and missed
> sessions. Rule-based planning; AI confined to explanation and chat.
>
> **RestOrTrain** — "CTL, ATL, TSB, zones, and load distribution … is **all
> calculated deterministically before the model sees anything**."
>
> **The STAS thread (61 replies)** — the diagnosed failure is "the **LLM making
> the calculations every time and sometimes differently**." Conclusion: move
> the maths outside the LLM and feed it prepared context.
>
> **Section 11** — 100 replies and 7 000 views for a _protocol_ whose entire
> value proposition is deterministic, auditable, reproducible coaching.

recover's pure modules (`volume.ts`, `athlete-level.ts`, `feasibility.ts`,
`materialize.ts`) behind an LLM coach that reads them through 63 tools is what
four independent teams concluded is the right shape.

**Action:** this is currently an implementation detail. It should be a stated
product claim.

## 3. Two competitors are shipping bugs recover is already immune to

**Availability treated as a target rather than a ceiling.**
LeCoach: "System treated daily hour inputs as targets rather than maximum
capacity." IntervalCoach: an athlete asked for more hours and the target
escalated 7.5 → 8.2 → 8.4 → 8.9 h/week; the developer flagged it high-priority
as "affecting the plan's credibility." `week-plan/volume.ts:1-12` exists to
prevent exactly this, and says so.

**No capacity calibration.** LeCoach opened plans at 15.5 h and 18.5 h for an
athlete who asked for moderate loading. RaceMind opened at 4 h/week for an
athlete already training 9. Both are what `athlete-level.ts`'s rolling peak
solves — and Domestique arrived at the same answer independently: "volume
capping based on recent training load rather than available hours."

**One number, many surfaces.** IntervalCoach shows 28 TSS on the calendar and
98 in the detail view. `assembleWeeklyTarget` was built to make that
structurally impossible.

## 4. Churn is configuration burden, not missing features

The only departure quote in the whole survey, from a long-term IntervalCoach
user: _"ça me prend plus de temps que de configurer mon plan moi-même."_ It
takes more time than planning it myself. Another user: "too complicated."

Nobody left because a feature was missing. Meanwhile the products that _are_
feature-rich are losing trust on arithmetic: Coach Watts is shipping Sweetspot
at **50 % FTP** instead of 85–95 %, and MTB plans containing walking.
Domestique shipped "45 min @ Z7" workouts and silently rewrote athletes' FTP.

**Design rules that follow:**

1. A plan preview is **confirm-or-adjust with at most three decisions**, never
   a wizard. intervals.icu's twelve-parameter Targets Generator produced six
   separate forum posts of people unable to work out why they got 16 weeks
   instead of 23.
2. Constants get **sources and visible explanations, not sliders**.
3. Breadth never lands before the arithmetic under it is right.

## 7. What recover leads on today

1. **Availability as a first-class ceiling.** intervals: "Not yet. We are
   working on athlete availability." IntervalCoach users are asking for it now
   — shift workers wanting weekly resets, a triathlete wanting "swim only on
   Wednesdays." recover has defaults, date overrides, blocks and a weekly
   confirmation prompt.
2. **Self-hosted with the athlete's own key**, including a local model.
3. **Proactive push** — athletedata's entire $299/yr proposition.
4. **One number across every surface.**

## 8. Corrections owed to existing documents

**`docs/specs/2026-07-28-training-volume-evidence.md` rates `HEADROOM = 1.3`
and `RAMP_CLAMP_PCT = 0.2` at High confidence, anchored to the acute:chronic
workload ratio safe zone.** That anchor no longer holds:

- Impellizzeri et al. 2020 (IJSPP), _Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio: Conceptual
  Issues and Fundamental Pitfalls_ — no evidence supports ACWR for load
  management; the ratio is mathematically coupled (the acute week sits inside
  the chronic window, producing spurious correlation) and its time windows are
  arbitrary.
- The 2025 literature is still contested, with recent work arguing the model
  "should be discarded as a framework."
- Separately, `HEADROOM` is **not an ACWR at all**. An ACWR is acute 7-day ÷
  chronic 28-day rolling load. `HEADROOM` is this week's hours ÷ a 12-week
  rolling _peak_. It reuses the number without inheriting the definition.

The values may well stay. The confidence rating and the justification must
change: 1.3 is defensible as "30 % above this athlete's own 12-week peak", an
empirical guard-rail, not a validated injury threshold. **Confidence: Low.**

**`periodize()` has no evidence document at all.** Its constants — 8 %/7 %/2 %
progression, 60 % recovery weeks, a 40/30/15 % phase split, a taper that
contradicts itself — have never been written down or sourced. See §9, v0.45.

**If we ever cite the 3:1 mesocycle, do not cite Issurin.** Issurin 2010 is
_block_ periodization: 2–4 week concentrated blocks in accumulation →
transmutation → realization. That is a different model. intervals.icu cites it
for 3:1 step loading, which it does not support. A targeted search for a
head-to-head comparison of 3:1 versus 2:1 in endurance athletes returns
nothing. **3:1 is coaching convention with no comparative evidence, and should
be labelled as such.**

## 9. Sequenced roadmap

Correctness first, then the cockpit, then breadth. Full breadth is in scope as
a goal; it is sequenced last because the market's own complaints are all about
arithmetic, not coverage.

Fuelling leads the breadth phase because MacroBurn proves it can be done free,
and it is the largest genuine hole in recover's coverage.

**The v0.45 row's original "dead skeleton taper" framing was this survey's
assumption, not a finding**: reading the code during v0.45 found
`materializeWeek` tapers only a priority-A race, so for a B or C race the
skeleton taper was the _only_ taper that existed, and it was repointed at
`race/taper.ts` rather than deleted for exactly that reason.

**v0.43 and v0.44 were swapped after v0.42 shipped (2026-08-05).** F2's
vocabulary half went with it — `service.ts:496-499` now matches provider sports
through `providerSportAliases` rather than a raw `eq()`, so a cyclist's rides
book against their planned rides again. What remains is the missing third
branch: a day with a planned session completed as a _different_ sport still
matches neither branch and books nowhere. That is a real silent defect, but it
went from "every ride, every cyclist, compounding weekly" to "a cross-training
day", which no longer outranks a guard that catches whole classes of wrong
plan before they reach the athlete.
